# Keeping your data when you buy software

*A vendor-neutral checklist for transit agencies evaluating any operations
system — scheduling, CAD/AVL, paratransit/demand-response, APC, fare
collection.*

The moment you have the most leverage over how your data will flow is the
moment **before** you sign. After signing, every export becomes a change
order. This page is a set of questions to ask every bidder and a set of
clauses worth putting in the contract. It names no products and recommends
no vendor — it exists because agencies keep discovering, a year in, that
the data describing their own service is hard to get out.

Headway is an open-source platform and its interest here is plain: it works
better when your data is reachable. But every item below is worth having
even if you never run Headway or anything like it.

## The one-sentence version

**The data your service generates is your record, not the vendor's
product — and your contract should say so, in the same section that says
how you get it out, in what format, how often, and at what price.**

## Ask every bidder these five questions

1. **How do we get a complete extract of our own data — all of it, not a
   report?** Look for: scheduled bulk export (nightly or better) of
   trip-level and event-level records, in a documented, non-proprietary
   format (CSV, JSON, Parquet), delivered somewhere you control (SFTP, an
   object-storage bucket you own, or a folder on your network). "You can
   download reports from the portal" is not the same answer.
2. **Is there an API, and what does it cost?** Look for: documented REST
   API covering the same records as the bulk export, with credentials the
   agency controls and can rotate; published rate limits; **no per-call
   or per-seat charge for reading your own operational data.** Ask whether
   API access is in the base price or an add-on module.
3. **Which open standards do you support today — in production, not on a
   roadmap?** For demand-response and paratransit, the relevant ones are
   **GTFS-Flex** (describing the service) and **TIDES** (trip and
   passenger event records). For fixed route: **GTFS** and
   **GTFS-Realtime**. A vendor who exports a standard format has already
   solved the hard part for every downstream system you will ever buy.
4. **What happens to our data when the contract ends?** Look for: a
   defined exit extract (complete history, documented format, delivered
   within a stated number of days, at no additional cost), and a stated
   deletion timeline for their copy afterwards. Ask for the *format*, not
   just the promise.
5. **What is your data dictionary?** A vendor who can hand you field-level
   documentation of their export on request has an integration story; one
   who cannot will bill you for discovery later.

## Clauses worth having in the contract

- **Ownership**: the agency owns all operational, passenger and vehicle
  data generated by its service; the vendor is a processor, not an owner.
- **Continuous access**: bulk export and API access are included for the
  life of the contract at no incremental fee, with a defined minimum
  cadence and a defined maximum staleness.
- **No blocking of downstream use**: the agency may transmit its own data
  to any other system it chooses, including open-source software, without
  vendor consent.
- **Exit**: a complete extract in a documented format within N days of
  termination, plus deletion certification.
- **Change protection**: the vendor gives written notice (30–90 days) before
  changing export schemas or API contracts, so the agency's downstream
  reporting does not break silently.

## Two traps that are easy to miss

**The on-premises to cloud regression.** Agencies replacing a system whose
database sat on their own servers often discover afterwards that the new
cloud product gives them *less* access to their own records than the old
one did — not because a vendor is hostile, but because nobody wrote the
export requirement down. If your current system lets your analysts query a
database directly, treat "how do we keep an equivalent level of access?"
as a scored evaluation question, not an afterthought.

**Reporting features are not data access.** Many products help prepare
National Transit Database submissions, and that is genuinely useful. It is
also scoped to the service that product runs. Your agency reports across
*all* of its modes and vendors, and the numbers must be defensible together
— so you still need the underlying records, in your hands, in a form you can
combine, audit, and explain years later when someone asks where a figure
came from.

## If you are evaluating right now

The evaluation itself is free leverage: **answers given during a
procurement are on the record**, and bidders answer questions they would
charge to answer later. Send the five questions above to every bidder in
writing, score the answers, and keep them with the award file.
